Privacy Policy

Last updated: 12 August 2026

This website is almost as quiet. It sets no cookies, runs no analytics and loads nothing from other servers. The only personal data involved is the request your browser makes to our host, an email address if you choose to join the mailing list, and whatever you write to us yourself.

2. The app: everything stays on your phone

Spross is an offline app. It has no server behind it, and the code that would talk to one does not exist: the iOS and watchOS sources contain no network API of any kind, and the Android app does not even declare the INTERNET permission, which means the operating system itself blocks it from opening a connection.

What is stored, and where

Your cards, your answers, your review history and your settings are written to your own device only:

  • on iPhone and Apple Watch, into the shared app container group.net.spross.app, which is what lets the widget and the watch app show the same box as the phone;
  • on Android, into the app's private storage.

We never see any of it. Deleting the app removes this data from the device along with it, and there is no copy anywhere else for us to delete on request — which also means the app itself is your only backup unless your device makes one (see below).

What the app does not do

  • no user accounts, no login, no email address, no name;
  • no analytics, usage statistics or telemetry;
  • no crash or diagnostics reporting to us;
  • no advertising, no advertising identifier, no tracking of any kind;
  • no third-party SDKs — the only library the app links is our own learning engine;
  • no camera, microphone, location, contacts or photo access; the app requests no permissions at all.

Between your own two devices

iPhone and Apple Watch keep each other up to date through Apple's WatchConnectivity, which passes the data directly between the two paired devices over Bluetooth or the local network. It does not travel over the internet and it does not reach a server of ours or of Apple's.

Saying words out loud

Recorded pronunciations ship inside the app and are played from it. Where no recording exists, the word is spoken by your device's own speech synthesis:

  • iOS/watchOS synthesises on the device. Spross only uses voices already installed; it downloads nothing. If you install a higher-quality voice, iOS itself fetches it from Apple, outside the app.
  • Android hands the word to whichever text-to-speech engine you have installed. Some engines, including Google's, may synthesise over the network, in which case the word being practised is sent to that engine's provider under its own privacy policy. Spross makes no such request and cannot observe it — but we would rather name it than let you assume otherwise. Your Android speech settings decide which engine is used.

Device backups

The app sends nothing, but your device's backup may carry the app's data along with everything else:

  • iOS includes the app container in an iCloud or encrypted local backup if you have backups switched on.
  • Android currently allows Auto Backup, so Android may copy the app's data into your Google Drive.

Both are your device's own function, under your setting and under Apple's or Google's terms — not a transmission by Spross. Turning the backup off, in the operating system's settings, stops it.

3. Getting the app: Apple, Google and GitHub

Spross is distributed outside the app stores at the moment: the Android package is downloaded from our GitHub release page, and the iPhone build reaches testers through Apple's TestFlight or as a direct over-the-air install.

Whoever hands you that download processes data about it — your IP address, the request, and with Apple an account and device identity — as their own controller, under their own privacy policy, not ours. That is true of Apple Inc. and GitHub, Inc. today, and it would equally be true of the App Store or Google Play, including any purchase data, if Spross is ever offered there: that processing is Apple's or Google's, not Polygon GmbH's.

Two things do reach us through those channels, and you should know about them:

  • if you take part in the TestFlight beta, Apple shows us the email address you joined with, which builds you installed, and any feedback or crash report you choose to send through TestFlight. We use that only to run the beta and answer you.
  • download counts from GitHub and Apple, which are aggregate numbers and identify nobody.

4. This website (spross.net)

Server logs

spross.net is a set of static files with no application server behind them. Like every web server, the host records each request: IP address, date and time, the file requested, the status and size of the response, and the browser's user-agent and referrer. This is processed to deliver the page and to keep the server secure and functioning, on the basis of our legitimate interest under Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, and is kept only for the short period the host needs for that purpose. Our hosting provider processes these logs on our behalf as a processor under Art. 28 GDPR.

No cookies, no analytics

The site sets no cookies. There is no analytics, no tag manager, no tracking pixel and no advertising. Every file the page loads — stylesheet, script, images, sounds, the learning engine — comes from spross.net itself; no fonts, no CDN and no other external resource is fetched, so no third party learns that you visited. That is also why you are not asked to consent to anything on arrival: there is nothing to consent to.

The counting drill

The drill on the landing page is the app's real engine compiled to JavaScript, and it runs entirely inside your browser. The numbers, your answers and your mistakes are never sent anywhere.

The drill stores one thing in your browser's local storage: your best streak per language, under keys of the form spross.record.numbers.de. It is a number, it contains no identifier, it is never transmitted, and it is only read to tell you when you beat it. We regard this as storage strictly necessary to provide the function you asked for (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG); clearing the site data in your browser removes it.

Speech in the browser

If your browser offers a voice for the language, the drill can read an answer aloud using the browser's built-in speech synthesis. Whether that happens on your device or through your browser or operating-system vendor's service depends on that vendor, not on us; we send nothing ourselves.

The mailing list

The signup form is not connected to a mailing-list provider yet. Submitting it today transmits nothing at all — it only shows a note asking you to write to us instead.

When the list opens, the address you enter will be used solely to send the Spross newsletter, on the basis of your consent under Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, confirmed by a double opt-in email. The list will be run by a specialist email service provider as our processor under Art. 28 GDPR; we will name that provider here before any address is transmitted to it. You can withdraw your consent at any time, with effect for the future, through the unsubscribe link in every issue or by writing to us, and the address is deleted when you do.

Writing to us

If you email feedback@spross.net, we process your address and whatever you write in order to answer you — Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR where it concerns the app you use, otherwise Art. 6(1)(f). Our email is hosted by a provider acting as our processor under Art. 28 GDPR. We keep correspondence as long as the matter is live and any statutory retention period runs, then delete it.
